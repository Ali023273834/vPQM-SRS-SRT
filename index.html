Each component earns full points at or better than its ideal, falls linearly to 25% of its points at the threshold and scores 0 beyond it (PQM value functions, Nelms et al.). Ideals for brain dose, conformity, GI and QRTOG are the bin medians of fully optimised atlas plans of the same volume and shape (brain dose also by scheme and location), interpolated in log-volume. Only the selected conformity index is scored; the other is ignored.

What to look at

    About this tool

    What it is. The vPQM is a plan quality assurance and assessment tool for intracranial stereotactic radiosurgery (SRS) and hypofractionated stereotactic radiotherapy (3 and 5 fractions). Conventional checks compare every plan with fixed thresholds such as CI < 1.2 and GI < 3, although the conformity and gradient that can be achieved depend strongly on target volume and shape. The vPQM instead compares each plan with what fully optimised plans of the same volume and shape achieved in a reference atlas of 367 saturated VMAT plans, while safety limits (normal-brain dose, OAR dose, coverage) stay absolute. The result is a single score with a component breakdown that shows where a plan falls short of what its geometry allows.

    How to use it in QA. Enter the metrics of a finished plan from your planning system and choose the conformity index your department reports — the RTOG index (PIV/TV) or the Paddick index (TVPIV²/(TV×PIV)) recommended by ICRU Report 91. A score above 85 means the plan reaches the achievable benchmark for its geometry; 75–85 means it is acceptable but one or more components lie below the benchmark and should be reviewed; below 75 suggests re-optimisation is likely to improve it; a red banner means a hard constraint is exceeded. Use the breakdown to decide where improvement is possible (for example the gradient or the minimum target dose), and record the score alongside your usual plan checks.

    What it is not. The vPQM is a quality-assurance and research aid, not a medical device and not a basis for clinical decisions. It does not approve or reject plans: acceptance remains the responsibility of the treating physicist and radiation oncologist under local protocols. It sees only the values entered — it cannot assess re-irradiation or cumulative dose, OARs other than those entered, target delineation, dose calculation accuracy or deliverability. The benchmarks were derived on one planning system (Monaco, Elekta Versa HD, 6 MV FFF VMAT) at one institution; other techniques, systems or prescription conventions may achieve different values.

    Validation status and multi-institutional study

    In a single-institution development cohort of 41 clinical plans (13 returned for re-optimisation), the score discriminated returned from accepted plans with an AUC of about 0.91 with either conformity index, and no returned plan scored above 85. The 75 and 85 boundaries were set on this cohort and are being tested prospectively.     How the score is built

    Domain A — safety and coverage (55 points): normal-brain dose (20; ideal = achievable dose for scheme, location, shape and volume, threshold = limit: SRS V12Gy 10 cm³, 3 fractions V20Gy 20 cm³, 5 fractions V24Gy 20 cm³); OAR maximum dose (20; ideal = TG-101 small-volume dose, threshold = TG-101 maximum point dose; full points if no OAR is near); coverage D99 (15; ideal 95%, threshold 90%). Domain B — quality (45 points): conformity by CIRTOG (threshold = ideal × 1.41, at most 2.0) or Paddick CI (threshold = ideal ÷ 1.41, at least 0.50) (15); GI (threshold = ideal × 1.52) (15); QRTOG (ideal = bin median up to 0.90, threshold 0.80) (15). Thresholds equal the measured ratio between first-loop and fully optimised plans. Hard constraints: brain or OAR limit exceeded, D99 < 90% or HI > 1.2. A value equal to a limit is marginal and still compliant.
